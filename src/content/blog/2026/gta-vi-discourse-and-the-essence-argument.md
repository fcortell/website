*Grand Theft Auto VI* came out on Thursday, 19 November, thirteen years after the fifth one, and sold the way weather happens. [It is the most anticipated launch the medium has had.](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1035847/grand-theft-auto-6-launch-review-news) And within hours of the servers opening, the argument was no longer about the game. It was about a list of things the game no longer does.

The list, as far as I can reconstruct it from the feeds: Lucia, one of the two protagonists, is a woman. Early missions involve buying flowers and groceries for her, which some people have decided is a humiliation. The long-running option to hire a sex worker is gone. Weapons will not fire inside a gay nightclub. And in one opening sequence, Lucia leaves her child behind to go and rob a place, which has been framed as a moral failing of the writing rather than what it is: a crime story opening with a crime.

## We wanted new, we got new, we objected

The honest version of the last decade of GTA discourse is that everybody asked for the same three things: a new city, a new protagonist, and systems that did not feel like 2013. Rockstar delivered a fictional Florida, a two-hander between Jason and Lucia, and a world with more moving parts than any of its previous ones. [The Verge's launch piece](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1035847/grand-theft-auto-6-launch-review-news) describes a game that arrives after a wait long enough to become its own genre.

What nobody asked for, apparently, was for the protagonist to be someone other than a man, or for the relationship to have mechanics. “Relationship simulator” is one of the kinder things said this week. A Fox News panel [used the launch](https://variety.com/2026/gaming/news/gta-6-fox-news-clavicular-manosphere-lucia-1236953444/) to tell adults to grow up, which is the first time in living memory that a cable news desk has been the voice of reason about a video game. The loudest streaming voices went further, calling the whole thing cucked, which is the sort of word that tells you more about the speaker's week than about the game.

I do not think this is a conspiracy. I think it is the oldest pattern in fandom: we demand novelty, and then we use the only ruler we own — the last one we loved — to measure it.

## The thing nobody actually played

Let me be fair to the complaint, because there is a real one underneath the noise.

The prostitution mechanic had been in the series since 2001, [and it is gone in VI](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-prostitutes-hookers-strippers-where-are-npcs-rockstar/): for the first time in twenty-five years you cannot hire a sex worker. [Strip clubs remain](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/gta-6-removes-sex-worker-mechanic-strip-clubs-remain-1833292). So the change is not prudishness across the board; it is a decision about one specific interaction.

And here is my question, asked without malice: when was the last time you used it? Not the first time, when we were fifteen and it was funny because it existed. The last time. Because I remember what the mechanic actually was: drive up, watch a badly animated minute, lose a small amount of money, get out. It was a punchline, not a system. It was funny in 2001 because games were not supposed to do that, and it has been coasting on that joke ever since.

Meanwhile the same game ships with a city you can read like a map, a relationship you can actually spend time on, and a version of being a bastard that has consequences instead of a cutscene. If the removal of one twenty-five-year-old gag is what counts as the essence of the series, then the essence was thinner than the marketing suggested.

## Being the villain is not the same as being a checklist

Let me say the part that will annoy the people who agree with the first half of this post: I like being a menace in these games. I like stealing a car because it is there. The series is a satire of American appetite, and appetite includes the ugly parts. Removing every uncomfortable corner would leave a very pretty driving game.

But “you can still be a criminal” and “every criminal option must be preserved forever” are not the same sentence. You can hold up a shop, outrun the police, wreck a bridge. You cannot shoot your way through one nightclub, and if you try, [the game kills you for it](https://www.attitude.co.uk/life/gta-6-protect-gay-nightclub-from-in-game-shootings-540134/). Given that Leonida is Florida and that Florida is where Pulse happened in 2016, that is not a mysterious decision. It is the one place where a joke stops being funny and starts being a headline about the player base. Rockstar has spent twenty-five years being blamed for what its players do; declining to animate one specific massacre is the cheapest possible way to grow up.

The rest is still there. The satire is still there. It is just pointed at different people now, and some of the people it is pointed at are the ones writing the complaints.

## Has GTA lost its essence?

Maybe. But I suspect what people mean by “essence” is “the version of this game that was new when I was sixteen”. Every generation gets one GTA that defines the word for them, and then measures everything after it in the wrong units. For some it is Vice City's soundtrack, for others San Andreas' sprawl, for a lot of people it is V's online mode, which is a completely different kind of thing.

What the series actually sold was never a checklist of vices. It was the feeling of a place that keeps going whether you are in it or not, plus a story you want to see the end of. That is closer to a novel than to a toy box, and novels do not owe you the same chapter every time.

Which is also why I do not need to be the same person in every one of them. My own taste is boringly consistent: I usually pick the male lead, in games and in books. But a choice is not a threat. If someone wants to play Lucia, she should get the same quality of writing, the same best missions, the same ending weight — not a reskin handed to her to keep the marketing department quiet. From what I have played, she does. That is not a concession to anyone. It is just better craft.

## What I actually want from GTA

Something to do that I have not done. A city that rewards looking. A story I would read if it were a book, with characters I can defend and characters I cannot. A reason to drive somewhere for fifteen minutes without a mission marker.

If that arrives with a woman in the lead and without a twenty-five-year-old gag about prostitutes, I will take it. The alternative — the same game, the same joke, the same protagonist, forever — is not “saving the essence”. It is buying the same postcard every year and calling it a holiday.
